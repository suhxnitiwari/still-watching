01 · Why it was needed

Growth stalled while a third of viewers paid nothing

In April 2022 Netflix reported its first subscriber loss in over ten years and, in the same letter, put a number on the problem: more than 100 million households were watching on a shared account, over 30 million of them in the U.S. and Canada.

Raising prices had limits, and new markets were saturating. The biggest pool of potential customers was already watching.

Problem statement. How might we turn engaged non-paying viewers into paying members without driving away the people who already pay?

02 · Measuring the need

Who is in the house, and who isn't

💳

The owner

Pays. Shares with an ex, a cousin, a college roommate. Doesn't want to be the bad guy.

Job: keep my people watching without a fight.

🫥

The borrower

Lives elsewhere and watches a lot. Has a profile, a history, a list.

Job: keep my shows. Pay as little as possible.

🎓

The away member

Part of the family, living somewhere else part of the year: college, travel, two homes.

Job: still feel like I'm home.

📈

Netflix

Needs revenue growth, not just reach, as the market matures.

Job: monetize sharing, protect retention.

The away member is the persona the policy handled least clearly. Netflix says everyone living in a household can watch anywhere. A college student lives somewhere else nine months a year.

03 · What shipped

Four pieces, tested market by market

Household detection

IP address, device IDs and account activity decide which devices belong to the home. Others get a verification prompt.

Extra Member

The owner pays to add someone outside the home: $2.99 in the first tests, $7.99 a month in the U.S.

Transfer Profile

Borrowers move their profile, history and list to a paid account of their own. This lowers the cost of switching.

Manage Access & Devices

Owners see every signed-in device and can sign one out. It gives them control and a reason to clean up.

The rollout

    Netflix dropped "Add a Home", tested in Argentina and four other markets, after customer feedback. Iterating in small markets before the U.S. kept the risk contained.

    05 · A user of one

    What it did to me

    I'm the away member. My family's account lives in Dallas; I moved to Austin for UT in August 2024. I never bought an Extra Member and never opened my own account. I just watched less, except when I was home.

    The insight. For Netflix's dashboard, I'm a retained subscriber. For engagement, the metric Netflix now calls its "best proxy for customer satisfaction", I'm a big drop during the school year. Paid sharing optimized for membership and quietly taxed the engagement of everyone who lives in two places.

    06 · How I'd have done it

    "Away at School"

    Keep paid sharing. Add a verified status for household members who live away part of the year.

    • Verify once: a .edu email, renewed each school year.
    • Stay linked: the student's devices stay in the household for the school year. No Extra Member fee, or a student price.
    • Use the pattern: devices that come home every summer and winter break are household devices. My data shows the pattern clearly.
    • Hand off at graduation: one tap to Transfer Profile into a first account of their own, with a discount.

    Why it pays off: today's 19-year-old on mom's plan is tomorrow's account owner. Keeping them engaged now protects a future subscription and the habit behind it.
